# Confidence

Confidence describes the strength of an **institutional conclusion** relative to the available testimony.

Confidence is not a property of a Witness.

Confidence is not a property of a phenomenon.

Confidence is not a claim made by a recognizer about its own correctness.

A Witness provides testimony.

The Registrar establishes its provenance.

The Interpreter evaluates testimony and determines how strongly the available evidence supports an interpretation.

---

## Testimony Is Not Confidence

A Witness may provide:

```text
SpeciesNameWitness
"SNEASEL"
```

That testimony does not need to say:

```text
confidence = 1.0
```

The Witness does not know whether it is correct.

It does not compare itself with other Witnesses.

It does not reason about the significance of its testimony.

It simply provides the best account of its experience.

---

## Confidence Belongs to Interpretation

The Interpreter may receive:

```text
SpeciesNameWitness
"SNEASEL"

TypeIconWitness
"DARK"

BattleCryWitness
matching Sneasel cry

FastMoveWitness
"SHADOW CLAW"
```

These remain separate testimonies.

The Interpreter may determine that together they provide sufficient support for:

```text
Opponent Species A = Sneasel
```

**That conclusion may have confidence.**

The individual Witnesses do not.

---

## Primary and Supplemental Testimony

Some interpretations have a preferred primary testimony.

For example:

```text
SpeciesNameWitness
"SNEASEL"
```

may provide the primary basis for identifying a species.

Other Witnesses may provide supplemental testimony:

```text
BattleCryWitness
TypeIconWitness
FastMoveWitness
MoveAudioWitness
SuperEffectiveWitness
```

If the primary testimony satisfies the required threshold, interpretation may proceed.

If it does not, supplemental testimony may strengthen the interpretation.

Conceptually:

```text
Primary support
       ↓
     (.x)
       │
       ├── sufficient → interpret
       │
       └── insufficient
                ↓
        supplemental testimony
                ↓
           (.y + .y + .y)
                ↓
          sufficient support
                ↓
             interpret
```

The important part is that **the Witnesses never perform this combination**.

They testify independently.

The Interpreter evaluates the collection.

---

## Confidence Is Not Certainty

Confidence expresses how strongly the available evidence supports an interpretation.

It does not transform an interpretation into an immutable fact.

Additional testimony may:

* increase confidence
* decrease confidence
* reveal conflict
* invalidate an interpretation
* produce a new interpretation

The original testimony remains unchanged.

---

## Published Confidence

Once an Article is published, its interpretation and associated confidence become part of the published record.

They are not edited later.

If new testimony contradicts the published interpretation:

> **Create a new Article.**

Do not modify the published Article.

The Timeline therefore preserves the history of changing confidence rather than silently rewriting it.

---

### 

> **Testimony and interpretation are different constitutional things**.

A Witness can be extremely reliable and still produce mistaken testimony.



> **Confidence is the Interpreter's measure of how strongly available testimony supports an interpretation.**

Everything else can derive from that.

> **the Witness tells us what happened to it; the institution decides how much that testimony supports what we think happened.**

> **Confidence is not manufactured by assessment. Confidence is revealed through assessment.
